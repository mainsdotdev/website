You spend a lot of your day in Mains, with agents working beside you. So this release is less about what the agents can do, and more about the place you do it in: a calmer layout, an icon that's yours, a sidebar that stays the way you arranged it, and a search box that finds the line you had in mind.

Everything that used to crowd the top of the sidebar now lives in a slim rail along the left edge: Home, Search, Pulse and Connect up top (plus Tasks and Plugins in Code spaces), your spaces at the bottom, and Help and Settings beneath them. Hover any of them to see what it is.

The list panel only shows up where there's a list to show — your conversations, and Settings. Pulse, Tasks, Plugins and Connect get the whole window to themselves.

Mains now lets you choose how it looks in your Dock. Open **Settings → Appearance → App icon** and pick from forty: the classic mark, light and dark, five solid colours, and sixteen gradients.

Eight of the gradients come from Japanese colour palettes — Fiery Dawn, Golden Mist, Deep Sea, Purple Sunrise and the rest — and eight more are new in this release, from Emerald Aurora to Copper Canyon. Each comes two ways: the colour inside the mark, or around it.

The Dock changes the moment you click, and your choice is back in place the next time Mains opens, before the window even appears.

### Pin what you're working on

Pin a workspace from its menu and it moves into a **Pinned** group at the top of the sidebar, where it stays until you unpin it.

### Put things where they go

In Code, workspaces now group under their project by default, and both projects and workspaces can be dragged into whatever order makes sense to you. In Work and Chat, projects reorder the same way — or with `⌥↑` and `⌥↓` when your hands are on the keyboard. Mains remembers the order you left.

### One door for new projects

**Add Project** brings cloning, creating and adding into one dialog. Paste a Git URL, start a fresh project, or pick a folder that's already on your computer.

### Project settings

In Work and Chat, a project's name, icon and files now live together in **Project settings**. Drop files in, and every chat in the project can use them — including chats already under way, from their next message. Agents only open a file when they need it, and they can't change the originals.

The file explorer's search box now looks inside your files, not just at their names. Switch it to **Text**, start typing, and matches from every file in the workspace appear, grouped by file, each one highlighted.

The toggles beside the field match case, whole words, or a regular expression. Walk the results with the arrow keys and press Enter to land on that exact line. It runs on ripgrep, the same engine VS Code uses, and it respects your `.gitignore`, so `node_modules` and build output stay out of the way.

**⌘F** inside an open file got smoother too: on big files it waits for a pause in your typing, instead of redrawing every match on every key.

### Hand Codex a Mac app

In a Codex chat, type `@` and the start of an app's name. Your installed Mac apps show up alongside your files and plugins, marked **Computer use**. Pick one and Codex knows exactly which app you mean when it reaches for your computer.

### Apps that fill the window

An MCP App that's better big — a map, a board, a canvas — can now expand to fill the window, and **Return to chat** brings you back. Apps that ask to open full-screen do so from the start.

### Citations you can click

When Codex points to a file it used, you now get a chip with the file's name that opens it in the editor, instead of a line of markup in the middle of a sentence.

### Every image it makes

Images Codex generates now make it into the chat and your deliverables even when they come back without a file on disk, up to 32MB. And when Codex only *looks* at an image, you see a small thumbnail of what it checked rather than a full-size copy.

Running two agents in the same worktree used to muddle the **Edited N files** card: each turn claimed every change made while it was open, the other agent's included. Now each turn keeps only the files it actually touched.

If both agents changed the same file, the card says so — **Shared with a parallel run** — and Undo steps aside, so taking back one agent's turn never takes the other's work with it.

## Also in this release

- **Preview HTML files.** Click a link to a local `.html` file in a reply and it opens in the in-app browser as a preview, sealed off from the network.
- **Paste files into the composer.** Copy files in Finder, or grab a screenshot, and paste. They arrive as attachments rather than a line of text.
- **A roomier PR editor.** **Expand PR view** opens the pull request form in a larger window and keeps what you've typed when you close it.
- **Activity, turn by turn.** A workspace's activity lists file changes for each turn and marks the ones you undid.
- **Tables that scan.** Tables in replies get row lines, so long comparisons are easier to follow.
- **Focus comes back.** Closing a dialog returns you to exactly where you were.
- **Colours in Search.** Projects keep their icon colour in the `⌘⌥K` menu.
- **Interactive deliverables** show their file's icon with an *Interactive* badge.
- **Goodbye, Codex Personality.** The setting is gone; Codex answers in its own voice in every mode.
- **Fixes.** Drafts keep their file chips when you switch spaces, switching workspaces shows its conversation right away, and the tab strip no longer flickers on the way into an empty space.
- **Under the hood.** Claude Agent SDK 0.3.283 and Codex app-server protocol 0.157.
