## In short

- Direct mobile pairing requires no Mains account. The mobile app talks to **your own computer**, and that computer talks to the **AI provider you choose**, under your own account with that provider. Optional desktop Bridge sync is described below.
- Pairing puts exactly one credential on your phone — a device token for that Mac — and it lives in the iOS Keychain.
- The phone keeps a limited local copy of recent conversations, unsent text drafts and your preferences. Documents are downloaded temporarily for previews. **Settings → Forget this computer** removes the saved pairing, conversation snapshot and unsent drafts for that computer.
- Messages, selected photos and documents, chat context and tool results can be sent to the **AI provider you choose** through your computer. The phone asks for permission for each computer and provider before sending your first request. Those services may retain content under your account according to their own policies.
- Neither app contains analytics, crash-reporting or advertising SDKs. This website sets no analytics cookies.

## Who this covers

Mains is built by the Mains team ("we", "us"). This policy covers the website at mains.dev, the **Mains desktop app**, the **Mains mobile companion**, and optional **Mains Bridge** sync. It does not cover the AI providers, code hosts and other services you connect to Mains — they process what you send them under their own policies, linked below.

## Pairing your phone with your Mac

The mobile app is a companion for Mains running on your computer. Direct pairing connects the app to the addresses supplied by that computer; it does not require a Mains account or a Mains-hosted relay.

1. On the computer you enable a reachable HTTPS endpoint, for example through Tailscale HTTPS. The Mac shows a **pairing code** as a QR code. The code is single-use and expires.
2. The phone scans the code and presents it to the Mac directly. The Mac answers with a **device token** made for that phone alone, together with its own name and the addresses it can be reached at.
3. From then on the phone opens a connection to the Mac with that token. The Mac keeps a list of paired devices and can **revoke** any of them; a revoked phone is refused at its next connection and must pair again before it can reconnect.

Direct pairing sends the pairing code and device token to your computer, without routing them through a Mains-hosted service.

## What the phone stores, and where

**In the Keychain.** The pairing record: the name and addresses of the Mac, this phone's device id, the device token, the pairing date, and the app and protocol version running on the Mac. It is stored with the "accessible after first unlock" protection class, so the app can reconnect once the phone has been unlocked after a restart. It never leaves the Keychain except to open the connection.

**Recent conversations and preferences.** The app saves a limited local snapshot of recent conversations and the spaces, projects and workspaces it displays. This may include messages, tool-call history, file references, connection timestamps and selected conversation settings. Display preferences and your AI data-sharing choices are also stored on the device. Temporary live activity, pending decisions, provider catalogues and queued messages are held in memory rather than saved as a permanent history of their own.

**Unsent drafts.** The app saves unsent message text in protected local storage, scoped to the paired computer and conversation or new-chat draft. Sending or clearing a draft removes its saved text. Draft attachments and queued message content are not saved as part of that text draft. These saved draft files are excluded from device backups.

**Images, downloaded documents and attachments.** Images and supported documents are fetched from the paired computer when needed. Document previews use temporary files that the app removes when the preview closes or is replaced; this is not a persistent offline document library. Temporary files may also be cleared by the operating system. To keep a file, use the share action to save it to Files or another destination. Copies saved or shared outside Mains remain under the control of that destination and are not removed by forgetting a computer in Mains.

**Photos, camera and selected files.** Camera access is requested for scanning a pairing QR code or taking a photo you choose to attach. Opening the Photos attachment panel requests access to your photo library and displays only the photos allowed by your permission choice. Only photos you add are attached to a message; browsing the grid does not send those photos to an AI provider. The selected attachment is prepared for the message, and a photo stored only in iCloud may need to be downloaded by the system. The Files picker reads the documents you select. You can change photo and camera permissions in the device's Settings.

The mobile app does not use iCloud to synchronize conversations. Its direct connection is separate from optional Bridge sync on your computer.

## Between your phone and your Mac

The phone and the Mac talk over a direct WebSocket connection; the device token is presented in the handshake. What travels:

- **Mac → phone:** runs, transcripts, tool calls, approval requests, workspaces, projects, spaces, provider settings and model lists, skills and commands, and — on request — stored text diffs, images, supported output files and message attachments.
- **Phone → Mac:** the prompts you write, photos, documents and skills you attach, your answers to approval requests, model and run-setting changes, requests to stop or fork a run, and requests for images and files.

Use an HTTPS/WSS endpoint for encrypted pairing and conversation traffic. Tailscale can provide an encrypted connection between your devices, with Tailscale HTTPS adding TLS. Connection protection depends on the endpoint and network you configure; plain HTTP/WS endpoints, where available in other Mains configurations, do not provide TLS encryption. Direct pairing is not a Mains-operated content relay.

Direct remote control connects to **your own machines**, rather than routing the mobile connection through Mains Bridge.

## Optional Bridge sync on your computer

Bridge is a separate, optional service for synchronizing desktop chats, Atlas pages and files across your devices. It requires an account. If you enable it on the paired computer, content arriving from your phone may be included in that computer's sync according to your Bridge settings.

Bridge encrypts content on your computer before uploading it. The service carries and stores encrypted content; account keys and recovery codes are not sent to it in plaintext. The service can see account and delivery metadata, including your account email, device names, online activity, storage usage, routing identifiers, sizes and update times. Bridge uses Cloudflare infrastructure. See the [Bridge overview](/bridge) and [Cloudflare's privacy policy](https://www.cloudflare.com/privacypolicy/).

Direct mobile pairing does not require Bridge. Revoking a Bridge device stops its access to sync; it does not erase copies already saved on that device.

## What reaches the AI provider

Runs are executed on your Mac by the provider's own tooling, under your own account with that provider: **Claude Code** (Anthropic), **Codex** (OpenAI), **GitHub Copilot** (GitHub / Microsoft) or **Cursor** (Anysphere), or **OpenCode** with the model provider you configure there. Some tooling can route a request to another model provider. The recipient depends on the tooling and account settings you choose on the computer. To complete a request, your computer can send:

- your message and the chat history of the run;
- photos and documents you attach;
- the skills and instructions you attach;
- the files, workspace context and tool results the agent reads or produces while it works;
- Mains' own instructions for the mode the run is in, and a short request to generate a title for the run.

External AI services process content under your account with them. They may retain prompts, attachments, responses and associated account information for longer than the request itself. Retention, deletion and any use for training depend on the provider, product and account settings, and are not controlled by Mains. See the policies for [Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/legal/privacy), [OpenAI](https://openai.com/policies/privacy-policy/), [GitHub](https://docs.github.com/en/site-policy/privacy-policies/github-general-privacy-statement), [Cursor](https://cursor.com/privacy). For OpenCode, also review the policy of the model provider you configured. We do not sell your prompts or attachments or use them for advertising.

On the phone, the first time you send to a given provider from a given Mac, Mains shows an **AI data sharing** sheet listing exactly this. Your first task request is not sent to that provider from the phone until you tap **Allow & Send**. Your decision is stored on the phone, per computer and provider, and can be reviewed or withdrawn in **Settings → AI Data Sharing**. Withdrawing it prevents new requests until you consent again; it does not delete data already received by an AI provider. Use that provider’s controls for its retained data.

## Other connections from your phone

Skill and plugin artwork may be loaded from HTTPS image addresses supplied by your computer’s catalogue. Those image hosts receive ordinary network request information, such as your IP address, but the app does not send them your chat history or pairing token. Links to privacy policies, support or other websites open at your request; those sites apply their own privacy and server-log practices.

**Demo Mode** uses bundled sample conversations and simulated activity on the phone. It does not contact a computer or AI service and does not grant real provider consent. Opening an external link or choosing to share a sample file uses the destination you select.

## Other connections your Mac makes

- **Code hosts and trackers you connect** — GitHub, GitLab, Linear, Jira, Asana, Notion, Slack and others. Their credentials are stored on the Mac and used only to fetch or link the items you ask for.
- **Update checks.** The Mac app checks Mains' GitHub releases for new versions; that request carries your app version and platform.
- **Tailscale**, if you turn it on, under [Tailscale's policy](https://tailscale.com/privacy-policy).

## Deleting your data

**On the phone.** Settings → **Forget this computer** removes that computer’s saved pairing record and AI-sharing choices, clears its synchronized local conversation snapshot and in-memory session state, and removes its saved unsent text drafts. It closes any open file preview and removes the associated temporary preview file. Reconnecting requires a new pairing. Removing a cached conversation or forgetting a computer on the phone does not delete the original conversation on your computer, any exported copies, or content already sent to an AI provider. Deleting the app removes its local app-container data; to explicitly remove pairing credentials, use **Forget this computer** before uninstalling. Files you exported must be deleted from their destinations separately.

**On the Mac.** Revoke a paired phone in the remote-access settings. Runs can be archived or deleted in the app; the app's own data lives in its Application Support folder. Removing the application alone does not necessarily remove that data. What you have already sent to an AI provider is held by that provider under its own policy.

## Analytics, tracking and advertising

**The apps:** no analytics SDK, no crash reporter, no advertising identifier and no fingerprinting. Direct mobile pairing communicates with your own computer. Optional Bridge account and sync requests communicate with the Bridge service as described above.

**The website:** we use [Cloudflare Web Analytics](https://www.cloudflare.com/web-analytics/) to count visits. It is cookieless: it does not set cookies, use browser storage or fingerprint you, and it does not track you across sites. Cloudflare receives the visited page (without query strings), timestamp, referrer, browser and device type, and page-load performance timings. See [Cloudflare's privacy policy](https://www.cloudflare.com/privacypolicy/). Our hosting provider also keeps ordinary server logs (IP address, user agent, requested page) for security and operations.

**When you write to us** — at team@mains.dev or through a form on this site — we receive what you send, such as your email address, and use it only to reply.

## Security

Every phone gets its own token; tokens live in the Keychain; the Mac can revoke any device at any time; pairing codes are single-use and expire; the computer validates a device token for authenticated mobile requests. For access beyond your home network, use Tailscale HTTPS. If you find a vulnerability, write to team@mains.dev and we will respond as quickly as we can.
